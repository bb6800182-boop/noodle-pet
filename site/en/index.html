A desktop pet for Windows

Lamian

After too long in your seat, it pops up, gets you stretching and drinking water, shows you the exercise and times it.

Why I made it

The original is Pete Cybriwsky (Instagram, opens in a new tab) 's Noodle (opens in a new tab): a little noodle that reminds you to stretch and demonstrates the exercise itself.

Lamian is its Windows desktop pet version: it stays in a corner of the desktop or walks along the taskbar, and pops up when it's time. It adds water reminders and custom reminders, and watches Google Calendar so it leaves you alone in meetings.

What it does

Everything is under Settings in the right-click menu and is saved automatically.

Stretch reminders

After 30 minutes of computer use in a row (adjustable) it calls you. Press Start to follow along while it times you; if you missed it, click the pet to bring it back.

It shows you how

Eight stretches: the pet demonstrates while the bubble explains the steps. Pick a focus and reminders rotate through exercises that suit it.

Water reminders

Every 60 minutes by default it drinks a glass next to you; press “Drank it” to log one. Missed it? Right-click and choose “Had a glass of water”.

Custom reminders

Set a time and repeat (every day, weekdays, chosen days or once) and the pet says your line in a speech bubble.

Google Calendar (optional)

It leaves you alone in meetings and pops up 10 minutes (adjustable) before an event. It reads through your own Apps Script, read-only.

Knows when to stay away

Five minutes without keyboard or mouse counts as a break. It stays away during calls and full-screen apps, and you can set quiet hours.

Claude Code (optional)

When Claude is busy and you have been sitting for 20 minutes or more, the pet calls you early so you move while you wait.

Screens

Reminders appear as speech bubbles next to the pet. The screenshots show the Chinese interface; the app is in English when Windows is not set to Chinese.

Download and run

  1. Download noodle-pet.exeIf the browser says the file is not commonly downloaded, choose Keep. There is nothing to install; put it in a permanent folder (such as Documents) before running it, so “Start with Windows” can find it later.
  2. First runWindows may show “Windows protected your PC”: click “More info” → “Run anyway”. This appears because the program has no paid code-signing certificate.
  3. Watch the introThe pet drops in from the top of the screen, shows you around and checks your desk setup.
  4. Right-click the pet → SettingsSet the reminder interval, pick a character and a stretch focus, and turn on Start with Windows.
  5. (Optional) Link Google CalendarUnder Settings → Google Calendar, three steps: copy the code, paste it into Apps Script in your own account and deploy, then paste the URL back.

Questions

What do I need?

Windows 10 or 11. It uses the .NET Framework 4.8 built into Windows, so there is nothing else to install.

Is it heavy on resources?

No. Idle, it uses about 0.5% CPU and about 80 MB of memory. Identical animation frames are merged, so it uses almost no CPU when still.

Where is my data? Is it uploaded?

Settings and logs are stored on your computer (%USERPROFILE%\.noodle-pet). No analytics, nothing uploaded.

Google Calendar is read through Apps Script in your own account, read-only, and the key stays on your computer. The Claude Code link only talks to this machine (127.0.0.1).

How do I remove it?

Turn off Start with Windows under Settings → General, right-click the pet and choose Quit, then delete noodle-pet.exe and the %USERPROFILE%\.noodle-pet folder. If you linked Google Calendar, also delete calendar-link.json next to the exe.

Is this the official Noodle?

No. Lamian is an unofficial Windows port, not affiliated with or endorsed by the author of Noodle. The characters, exercise demos and instructions come from Noodle (MIT License); both license texts are under Settings → General → Licenses.

Are the stretches right for everyone?

The stretches are general activity suggestions, not medical advice. Stop if anything hurts or feels wrong; if you have an injury or condition, ask a doctor or physiotherapist first.
